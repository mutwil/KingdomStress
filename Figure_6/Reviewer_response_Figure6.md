> It is much useful if we can capture the cis-regulatory sequence features for predicting stress-responsive genes across green plants. However, the current auROC from different models for different stress exhibits relatively low, less than 0.8. I am not sure whether using training set from specific lineages or specific tissues could increase the auROC and better predict the stress-responsive genes. ## Response

We thank the reviewer for two constructive suggestions, which led us to add a new
supplementary figure (**Figure S11**) and seven new analyses. In short: lineage-restricted
training does improve performance, tissue restriction does not, and the discovered
regulatory signal is confirmed by independent experimental TF-binding data. We also
quantified how much of the remaining gap to auROC 1.0 is attributable to label noise
rather than model deficiency.

### 1. Would lineage- or tissue-restricted training raise auROC?

**Lineage restriction: yes, modestly but very consistently.** We retrained the interpretable
feature model separately within each of the three well-sampled lineages (Monocot, Rosid,
Asterid), and compared each lineage model against a **size-matched control** trained on all
species but subsampled to the same number of genes and the same positive rate. This control
is essential, because a lineage subset is also a smaller subset, and the two effects would
otherwise be confounded. Scoring was done on a fixed test set (that lineage's genes in the
original test split), so the arms differ only in training composition.

| Lineage | Δ auROC vs size-matched control | n |
|---|---|---|
| Asterid | +0.033 | 10 |
| Monocot | +0.019 | 10 |
| Rosid | +0.018 | 10 |
| **Overall** | **+0.023** | **30** |

Paired Wilcoxon *P* = 1.9 × 10⁻⁹ (**Figure S11C**). Lineage-restricted models therefore do
capture lineage-specific cis-regulatory grammar, and the effect is largest in the Asterids.
The magnitude (~0.02 auROC) means this is a refinement rather than a route to auROC > 0.8.

**Tissue restriction: no consistent gain.** We rebuilt the labels from scratch using only
Leaf experiments, and separately only Root experiments, recomputing DEG frequency and the
quartile thresholds within each organ (this changes the label definition, not merely the
training subset). Against an all-organ rebuild produced by identical code on the identical
gene universe:

- Leaf-only labels: **+0.003** mean Δ within-species auROC (n = 10; improved in 5 of 10 sets)
- Root-only labels: **+0.012** (n = 4; Root has usable data only for Drought and Salt)

(**Figure S11G**). The exception is instructive: Pathogen benefits most from Leaf-only labels
(+0.022 UP, +0.021 DOWN), consistent with pathogen assays being performed almost exclusively
on leaves, so the all-organ label adds noise rather than signal for that stress. For stresses
assayed across many organs, restricting to one organ removes as much signal as noise.

### 2. Why auROC is not higher than 0.8

Two analyses bound what is achievable with these labels.

**(a) Part of the published number reflects between-species prevalence, not biology.** The
DEG-frequency labels are quartile-based within each species, but they are zero-inflated: in a
shallowly assayed species most genes have frequency 0, which pushes the 25th percentile to
zero and unbalances the classes. The realised positive rate therefore tracks assay depth
(Spearman ρ = 0.771, *P* = 0.0034 against experiments per species; **Figure S11A**) and spans
up to 20.9-fold within a single stress-direction set (Salt DOWN: *Brachypodium* 0.031 vs
*Arabidopsis* 0.645). Because sequence composition is strongly species-diagnostic, a model can
gain apparent performance by inferring species and predicting its base rate.

Decomposing the published auROC into within-species and between-species components
(**Figure S11B**):

| Model | Pooled (published) | Within-species | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| PlantCAD2 | 0.736 | 0.717 | −0.019 |
| PlantRNA-FM | 0.737 | 0.715 | −0.022 |
| Fused | 0.755 | 0.735 | −0.020 |

The effect is real but small: ~0.02 auROC. The models genuinely discriminate **within**
species at 0.735 (fused). Equalising the positive rate across species during training
confirms the mechanism directly — pooled auROC falls in **10 of 10** sets (mean −0.015) while
within-species auROC rises in **10 of 10** (mean +0.005; **Figure S11D**). We now report the
within-species value alongside the pooled value throughout.

**(b) The labels themselves are only ~0.83 reproducible.** We split the bioprojects
contributing to each stress into two disjoint halves, recomputed DEG-frequency labels
independently in each half, and measured how well one half's frequency predicts the other
half's binary label (20 random splits per stress-direction). This is an empirical ceiling: the
score a perfect predictor of the underlying signal would obtain against labels this noisy.

Mean within-species ceiling = **0.833** (range 0.756 for Pathogen UP to 0.888 for Heat DOWN).
Against that reference the fused model attains **71%** of the achievable discrimination above
chance, ranging from 47% (Heat) to 84% (Pathogen) (**Figure S11E**). We are explicit that this
bounds agreement with *these* labels at *this* assay depth, and is not a bound on the
underlying biology.

Taken together, an auROC near 0.75 against labels that are themselves ~0.83 reproducible is
closer to the attainable limit than the raw number suggests, and we have revised the
Discussion to make this quantitative rather than apologetic.

### 3. Validation against independent experimental TF-binding data

We used the **experimental ChIP-seq** TF binding sites distributed by PlantRegMap (section 2.5;
67 peak files, 34 *Arabidopsis* TFs). We deliberately did **not** use PlantRegMap's
`TFBS_from_motif_*` files: those are motif-scan predictions, so testing our motifs against them
would be circular. Peaks were assigned to genes by peak **summit** within a promoter window
(−1000/+200 bp of the TSS, TAIR10/Ensembl 59); summit rather than span was used because some
peaks exceed 10 kb and span-based assignment would call nearly every gene bound.

Genes with an experimentally bound promoter are markedly more likely to be stress-responsive
in our held-out test set. Strongest associations (Fisher exact, BH-corrected):

| TF | Stress | Odds ratio | q | Responsive if bound vs unbound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NAC102 (AT5G63790) | Salt UP | 6.94 | 5 × 10⁻⁵⁸ | 81% vs 38% |
| NAC032 (AT1G77450) | Salt UP | 4.93 | 5 × 10⁻⁶¹ | 73% vs 36% |
| ABF4/AREB2 (AT3G19290) | Salt UP | 3.88 | 1 × 10⁻⁵² | 68% vs 35% |
| ABF1 (AT1G49720) | Salt UP | 3.78 | 8 × 10⁻⁴⁷ | 68% vs 36% |
| ATHB6 (AT2G22430) | Salt UP | 3.66 | 2 × 10⁻⁵⁶ | 65% vs 33% |
| RD26 (AT4G27410) | Pathogen UP | 3.54 | 3 × 10⁻⁵⁰ | 56% vs 26% |

**Specificity control.** Because bound promoters tend to be open and highly expressed, and
highly expressed genes are more often called DEG, enrichment could in principle reflect
promoter accessibility rather than stress regulation. We therefore compared TFs with
established stress roles (ABF1/3/4, DREB2A, HSFA6A, RD26, ATHB6/7, ZAT6, NAC032/102, MYB44)
against TFs whose primary roles are developmental or circadian (SEP3, AG, LFY, RGA, CCA1,
BBM, REF6, TRB1), and repeated the analysis restricted to high-confidence peaks:

| Peak set | Stress TFs | Developmental TFs | *P* |
|---|---|---|---|
| All peaks (median 5,833 bound genes) | 1.156 | 0.559 | 4.0 × 10⁻²⁴ |
| Top 2000 peaks, ≤3 kb (median 1,318) | **1.242** | **0.491** | 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ |

(mean log₂ odds ratio; Mann-Whitney, one-sided; **Figure S11F**). A generic accessibility
component clearly exists — developmental TFs sit at log₂OR ≈ 0.5 — but stress TFs are
substantially higher, and the separation **widens** when only high-confidence peaks are used
while the developmental baseline falls. This is the behaviour expected of genuine
binding-driven signal rather than an accessibility artefact.

We also examined the 21 TFs assayed both under ABA treatment and untreated. Enrichment did not
increase under ABA (mean Δlog₂OR = −0.059, *P* = 0.015), indicating that the signal comes from
which promoters these stress TFs target rather than from the treatment itself; we state this
explicitly rather than over-interpreting the condition contrast.

**Motif-level comparison.** For completeness we compared the 300 top-ranked discovered PWMs
against JASPAR2024 CORE plants (805 PFMs) using Sandelin-Wasserman similarity with a
within-column-shuffle null. Discovered PWMs score above null (median similarity 0.985 vs 0.955
for PlantCAD2), but the highest-scoring PWMs are low-complexity and A/T-rich (poly-A, poly-T,
TA-repeats) and match A/T-rich DOF/CDF/IDD-family motifs. We therefore do **not** claim
specific TF-motif correspondence from the PWMs; this is itself consistent with our conclusion
that compositional features rather than discrete motifs carry most of the predictive signal.
The evidence for known cis-elements rests on the k-mer analysis in Figure 6F and, decisively,
on the independent ChIP-seq enrichment above. We note as a limitation that our permutation
budget (20 null rounds) cannot support per-motif significance testing after correction across
300 comparisons, so we report effect sizes rather than *P* values for this comparison.

## Methods additions

**Within-species and between-species decomposition.** Per-gene test predictions were joined to
species labels and auROC recomputed within each species (≥200 test genes and ≥20 genes of each
class), then averaged weighted by test-set size. The difference from the pooled auROC measures
the contribution of between-species prevalence differences.

**Assay-depth audit.** Positive rate per species was compared against the number of distinct
experiments and bioprojects contributing to that species in the DEG table (Spearman correlation).

**Lineage-restricted training and size-matched controls.** Species were assigned to Monocot,
Rosid, Asterid, Caryophyllid, Gymnosperm, Lycophyta, Bryophyta or Chlorophyta. For each
lineage and stress-direction, three models were trained and evaluated on the identical test
set: all species; that lineage only; and all species subsampled to the lineage's training-set
size with matched positive rate (three independent draws). Learner:
`HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (300 iterations, max depth 6, learning rate 0.1), used in
place of the published `GradientBoostingClassifier` to make ~110 model fits tractable; the
unstratified baseline is reported for every comparison so the substitution is auditable.

**Organ-restricted labels.** DEG frequency and quartile thresholds were recomputed using only
experiments from a single harmonised organ. The organ field was harmonised conservatively
(case variants and sub-regions merged; multi-tissue and ambiguous values excluded), retaining
Leaf, Root, Seedling and Shoot. Split membership was inherited from the published splits so
that arms remain comparable. An all-organ rebuild produced by identical code agreed with the
published labels on **100%** of genes labelled by both, validating the reimplementation.

**Label-noise ceiling.** For each stress, contributing bioprojects were partitioned into two
disjoint halves; DEG frequency and quartile labels were recomputed independently within each
half; and the auROC of one half's continuous frequency against the other half's binary label
was computed within species and averaged (20 random partitions). Reported as an empirical
reproducibility ceiling for these labels at this assay depth.

**TF-binding validation.** Experimental ChIP-seq TFBS BED files for *Arabidopsis thaliana* were
obtained from PlantRegMap (section 2.5). Motif-derived TFBS predictions were excluded to avoid
circularity. Peaks were assigned to genes when the peak summit fell within −1000/+200 bp of an
Ensembl 59 TAIR10 TSS. Enrichment of stress-responsive genes among bound versus unbound
promoters was tested by Fisher's exact test with Benjamini-Hochberg correction, separately for
all labelled *Arabidopsis* genes and for held-out test genes. Specificity was assessed by
contrasting stress-associated against developmental/circadian TFs (Mann-Whitney) and by
repeating the analysis on the 2000 strongest peaks per TF (≤3 kb).

**Motif comparison.** Discovered PWMs were compared with JASPAR2024 CORE plants using
Sandelin-Wasserman similarity maximised over offsets (minimum overlap 5) and both strands. The
null was the best match against a size-matched database of PWMs shuffled within columns, which
preserves column information content and motif length. RNA-alphabet PWMs were converted to DNA.

## Separate corrections identified during this work

These are independent of the reviewer's comment but should be fixed before resubmission.

1. **Species count.** The Figure 6 training data contain **24** species, not 25 as stated. Each
   stress-direction set uses only 14–21 of them, and the species set differs between stresses.
2. **Lineage coverage.** Gymnosperms, lycophytes and charophytes are absent from the Figure 6
   data (they lack extracted gene sequences), although they are present in the wider atlas.
   Text describing the Figure 6 species panel should be corrected accordingly.
3. **Duplicated rice entry.** The DEG table lists `Oryza_sativa` (1,228,094 rows) and
   `Oryza_sativa_Japonica_Group` (773,908 rows) as separate species. Since labels are computed
   per species name, rice was labelled twice from partitioned data. Worth checking whether this
   affected the rice labels used in Figure 6.
4. **Prediction files.** The DNA and RNA per-gene prediction CSVs disagree on 6–52 labels per
   set (out of ~80,000). Immaterial to any result, but the cause should be identified.
